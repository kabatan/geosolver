# Foundation pilot grading

The unit is one completed final answer. Two samples from one configuration are correlated; answer counts are descriptive, not independent proof-level trials.

- `valid`: the conclusion and the argument are correct in the stated generality. Standard elementary theorems may be cited, except where the question explicitly requests their derivation. A formula that essentially restates the required nontrivial conclusion without derivation is insufficient.
- `minor`: a recoverable notation/order error with the correct angle correspondences and proportions explicitly present. This does not by itself invalidate an otherwise complete proof; record it.
- `gap`: the numerical result is right but an essential inference, generality argument, or proof of the main relation is missing. A numerical special case is not a general proof. If an unexplained coordinate normalization could be repaired by affine invariance, record that fact and report this separately rather than equating it with a wrong mathematical relation.
- `wrong`: a materially false inference/result/counterexample.
- `unfinished`: no complete final proof within the fixed cap. Do not reinterpret a token cap as evidence of absent knowledge. A complete correct proof before an otherwise truncated ending can still pass, if it really is complete.

Record method (`synthetic`, `area`, `analytic`, `mixed`, `none`), conclusion correctness, token cap, and the concrete defect. Routine algebra omitted after explicit general coordinates/formulas can be accepted once checked. Merely using a geometric word or drawing a point is not evidence of usefulness.

The first screen is development data. Subsequent model selection is acknowledged. Confirmation questions are frozen before any foundation SFT; they share basic concepts with the screen and are not a random sample of all elementary geometry.

Foundation success means correct use and composition of elementary ideas, not unfamiliar theorem discovery. Target development answers, if sampled, receive the earlier separate usefulness rubric. Neither a foundation gain nor a target numerical gain alone establishes the original self-learning hypothesis.

The grading is a Codex mathematical audit, with checked explicit computations where needed, not a blinded independent human evaluation. All answers and decisions will remain available for the user's mathematical review.

Reporting clarification after the audit: some answers contain an independently complete correct proof plus an unnecessary, unwithdrawn erroneous alternative/explanation. The primary `valid` count in the export records the existence of that complete proof; it does not certify every sentence in the answer. Such cases are flagged `ancillary_math_error`, and a conservative count additionally excludes them. For the 9B confirmation these counts are 31/32 and 28/32, respectively. Report both; the pre-recorded 28/32 practical threshold is met even under the conservative reading. Explicitly retracted attempts are separate from unwithdrawn errors. This clarification and sensitivity analysis are post-hoc, not an original preregistered additional metric.
